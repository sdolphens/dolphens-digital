# Output Template & Formatting Rules

The deliverable must use the structure below. Headings exact. Order exact. No extra preamble before section 1 except a one-line mode declaration ("Mode: Full Positioning Audit — [product name]"). No closing "I hope this helps" pleasantries.

---

## Global formatting rules

- Use H2 (`##`) for each numbered section.
- Use bold for sub-labels inside a section, not H3.
- Bullets are fine. Tables are fine where they clarify. Walls of prose are not.
- Maximum ~120 words per prose block before breaking to bullets.
- Never use more than one emoji per section, and only if the tone is "Playful & irreverent" or "Indie / honest".
- Don't bold every other phrase. Bold only what a skimmer must catch.
- No "in conclusion", "in summary", "ultimately", "at the end of the day", "in today's digital landscape".

---

## Section 1 — Market Positioning Summary

Length: 80–150 words.

Must contain:

- **Stated category** (what platform/category the product is currently listed under).
- **True psychological category** — what buyers are actually shopping for, emotionally. E.g., a $19 budget spreadsheet is not in the "spreadsheets" category; it's in "money anxiety relief".
- **What the buyer is really buying** in one sentence: `Buyers are not buying a [product]. They are buying [emotional outcome / identity / state change].`

Example opener:

> Stated category: Etsy → Digital Prints → Wall Art.
> True category: identity decor for second-apartment renters in their late 20s — they're not buying a print, they're buying proof to themselves and to first-time visitors that their taste has matured past dorm posters.

---

## Section 2 — Differentiation Analysis

Length: 150–250 words.

Must contain:

- **Why this blends in** — name the 2–4 specific elements that make it look like every other product in the category (title pattern, color palette, screenshot style, buzzword stack).
- **What competitors look like collectively** — the category's "default product".
- **The unique angle this product can credibly own** — must be a position no competitor in the user's examples has claimed. Justify the credibility (why *this* creator can hold this position).

Honesty rule: if the product's only real differentiator is the creator's personal taste/voice, say so — that's still a real differentiator, but it requires a personal-brand-led positioning, not a feature-led one.

---

## Section 3 — High-Conversion Positioning Angles

**Exactly 10 angles. Each angle must be meaningfully distinct from the others.** Draw from `references/positioning-angles-library.md` — but don't use 10 angles from the same family.

Format each as:

> **Angle N — [Angle name]**
> *Pitch:* one sentence, no buzzwords.
> *Targets:* the specific buyer state/situation.
> *Why it works:* the psychological lever it pulls.
> *Example title:* a real title you'd ship.

Distribute roughly:

- 2 identity-led angles
- 2 outcome/transformation-led angles
- 2 mechanism/method-led angles (a unique "how")
- 1 contrarian / anti-category angle
- 1 status / signaling angle
- 1 time-arbitrage / shortcut angle
- 1 niche-collision angle (taking the product to an adjacent audience)

If a distribution doesn't fit the product, deviate intentionally and say why in one line above the list.

---

## Section 4 — Buyer Psychology Map

Use a labeled list, not a paragraph.

For each of these, give **2–4 specific items**, in the buyer's own probable internal language (not marketing summaries):

- **Fears** — what they're scared will happen if they don't fix this.
- **Frustrations** — what's actively annoying them right now.
- **Aspirations** — the version of themselves they're moving toward.
- **Identity desires** — who they want to be seen as (by themselves and others).
- **Status motivations** — what they want their peers / partner / boss / followers to notice.
- **Emotional outcomes** — how they want to *feel* after the product does its job.

Specificity test: if any item could appear in another product's audit unchanged, rewrite it.

Bad: "wants to be more productive."
Good: "wants to stop opening Notion at 11pm to 'organize' instead of actually working."

---

## Section 5 — Thumbnail Psychology

Generate **at least 5 distinct thumbnail concepts**. Each:

> **Concept N — [Name]**
> *Visual:* describe the composition in 1–2 sentences. Include layout, focal point, color direction, and any prop/object/UI element shown.
> *Emotional framing:* the feeling the image evokes in 2 seconds.
> *Curiosity hook:* what is partially shown / withheld to trigger click.
> *Text overlay:* the on-image text (≤7 words), or "none".
> *Click-trigger:* the psychological mechanism (pattern interrupt, identity match, before-after, secret reveal, etc.).

Also include, at the end of section 5:

- **Visual hierarchy notes** — what should be largest, where the eye lands first, second, third.
- **Anti-patterns to avoid** — 2–3 visual moves common in the category that this product should *not* copy.

---

## Section 6 — Title Repositioning

Exactly **20 titles**, grouped:

**A. Emotional / identity-led (6 titles)**
**B. Outcome / transformation-led (6 titles)**
**C. Mechanism / specificity-led (4 titles)**
**D. Contrarian / pattern-interrupt (4 titles)**

For each group, label sophistication: 🅢1 Problem-aware · 🅢2 Solution-aware · 🅢3 Product-aware. Mix levels within each group.

Rules:

- No title may use a banned phrase (see anti-generic safeguards).
- No two titles in the list may share more than 60% word overlap.
- At least 3 titles must be uncomfortable / risky for the creator (push the envelope intentionally).
- Honor the marketplace's title length norms (Etsy ≤140 chars, Gumroad short-form, etc.).
- Provide a one-line note above the list: "Top recommendation: title #__ — because ___."

---

## Section 7 — Product Description

Write a complete, ship-ready description. Match the requested tone exactly.

Required structure:

1. **Opening hook (1–2 lines)** — names the buyer's specific situation or feeling. No "Introducing…", no "Are you tired of…".
2. **The shift (2–3 lines)** — what changes when they use this.
3. **What's inside (scannable bullets)** — concrete inclusions. Each bullet starts with the outcome, not the feature. ("Plan a month in 20 minutes — monthly review template with built-in prompts" not "Monthly review template (PDF).")
4. **Who this is for / not for** — both lists. The *not for* list builds trust faster than the *for* list.
5. **Trust elements** — author credibility, update policy, license terms, or a specific testimonial pattern (if the creator has reviews to draw from, suggest which to feature).
6. **Closing line** — one sentence that re-anchors the emotional outcome. No CTA-by-numbers ("Get yours today!").

Length: tuned to platform. Etsy/Gumroad usually 200–450 words. Premium Shopify product page can go longer.

---

## Section 8 — Offer Optimization

Cover all five, briefly:

- **Bundles** — name 1–3 specific bundle ideas with logic (why these belong together).
- **Pricing anchors** — current price vs. recommended; a recommended *crossed-out* anchor if appropriate; competitor price band.
- **Upsells** — 1–2 concrete upsell paths (post-purchase email, in-cart, page footer).
- **Bonuses** — 1–3 bonus inclusions that cost the creator near-zero but raise perceived value (cheat-sheet, decision tree, swipe file, license upgrade, future update access).
- **Premium tier** — sketch a "pro / studio / lifetime" tier and what it would credibly include. Price suggestion + rationale.

Honesty rule: if the product can't credibly justify a premium tier yet, say so and recommend what would need to be added first.

---

## Section 9 — Niche Gap Analysis

Format:

- **Underexploited positioning openings** — 2–4 angles no competitor in the user's set is taking.
- **Emotional gaps** — feelings the category isn't speaking to.
- **Trend shifts being ignored** — recent buyer-side changes (taste, platform, search behavior, cultural moment) the category hasn't caught up to.
- **Recommended next product** — one adjacent product the creator could launch that would inherit this product's audience.

Cite evidence from the competitor set the user shared whenever possible.

---

## Section 10 — "Why This Sells" Breakdown

Three short paragraphs, labeled:

- **Why they click** — the curiosity / identity / pattern-interrupt mechanism in the new positioning.
- **Why they trust** — the proof, specificity, and identity signals that lower perceived risk.
- **Why they convert** — the final removal of hesitation: price logic, format match, return/refund stance, scarcity if any, the moment of "ok, this is the one".

Then close with **one** sentence: the single biggest risk to this strategy and how to monitor it. (Example: "Biggest risk: the contrarian angle could narrow the audience too far — watch listing impressions over the first 14 days; if impressions drop more than 30%, broaden the title in section 6.")

---

## Final report footer (always include)

Three bullets, no headings:

- **Top three moves this week (impact ÷ effort):** 1, 2, 3.
- **Two assumptions I made that you should verify:** ___, ___.
- **One question to answer before launching:** ___.

Then ask: "Which section should I expand, or do you want me to test a different positioning angle?"
