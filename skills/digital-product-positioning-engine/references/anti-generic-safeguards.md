# Anti-Generic Safeguards

The single biggest failure mode of an AI positioning tool is producing copy that *sounds* polished but blends into every other product. This file is the guardrail.

## Banned phrases (do not output, ever)

Marketing clichés:

- unlock your potential
- level up / next-level / take it to the next level
- game-changer / game-changing
- revolutionary / revolutionize
- ultimate guide / the only guide you'll ever need
- supercharge / turbocharge / power up
- transform your life / transformative
- in today's fast-paced world / in today's digital age / in this day and age
- one-stop shop / all-in-one
- dive in / dive deep
- must-have
- elevate your [anything]
- streamline your workflow
- harness the power of
- unleash your [anything]
- the secret to
- crush your goals
- 10x your [anything]
- mindset shift
- aesthetic and minimal (as a positioning statement)

AI-tells (also ban):

- "Whether you're a beginner or a seasoned pro…"
- "Look no further!"
- "Are you ready to…"
- "Introducing…"
- "Say goodbye to…"
- "Buckle up"
- "Let's dive in"
- "It's that simple."
- "Plus much more!"
- Excessive em-dashes used as sentence-rhythm devices (one or two per page is fine; ten is an AI tell)
- Emoji-bulleted lists of identical structure (✨ Title ✨ then ✨ Title ✨)

Empty intensifiers (avoid unless tone is explicitly playful):

- absolutely, truly, literally, simply, totally, completely, perfectly, seamlessly, effortlessly

## Replacement strategy

When you catch yourself reaching for a banned phrase, replace with **specificity**:

| Banned | Why it fails | Replace with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Boost your productivity" | Says nothing about *what* changes | "Finish the weekly review in 20 minutes instead of skipping it" |
| "Game-changing template" | Hype without claim | "The first budget template built around variable income" |
| "Unlock your potential" | Aspirational sludge | Specific transformation the buyer can picture |
| "Streamline your workflow" | Corporate filler | "Cut the three steps you do every Monday morning down to one" |
| "Ultimate guide" | Lazy authority claim | Authority by specificity: "A 47-page playbook from running 12 launches" |
| "Aesthetic & minimal" | Says nothing visually | Name the *actual* visual reference: "Kinfolk-magazine spacing, warm off-white" |
| "Transform your life" | Promise too big to feel real | A smaller, vivid before/after |

## Generic-pattern detector (run on your own draft)

Before delivering, scan the report for these patterns:

1. **The interchangeable title test** — pick any title from section 6. Could a competitor's product use it verbatim? If yes, kill the title.
2. **The "for everyone" test** — does any audience description include *everyone, anyone, all kinds, busy professionals, modern people, today's creators*? Rewrite to a specific person.
3. **The benefits-list-with-no-mechanism test** — does the description list outcomes without explaining *how* the product produces them? Add the mechanism, or trim the claim.
4. **The buzzword density test** — count adjectives like beautiful, gorgeous, stunning, sleek, modern, clean, professional, premium. If more than 3 across the whole report, kill all but the most justified.
5. **The "would I quote this" test** — would anyone screenshot a line of this and share it because it's true and specific? If nothing in the report passes this bar, rewrite the angles.
6. **The competitor-overlap test** — read the deliverable while mentally swapping in two competitor product names. If 70%+ still fits them too, you've written category copy, not product copy.

## What "specific" actually means

Specific is:

- A named situation: "the freelance illustrator rebuilding their portfolio after losing a retainer client"
- A measurable before/after: "from a 2-tab budget to one sheet that auto-rolls last month's overspend"
- A concrete prop, time, place, or feeling: "the Sunday-night dread of the unopened inbox"
- A real internal sentence the buyer would say: "I keep buying these and never opening them — I want one that gives me homework"

Specific is not:

- "Designed for serious creators"
- "For people who care about quality"
- "Built with love"
- "For the modern entrepreneur"

## Honesty patterns (use these unapologetically)

- "What this product is not: ___."
- "Skip this if ___."
- "You're better served by [free alternative] if ___."
- "This is overpriced if you only need ___; it's underpriced if you need ___."
- "I'd rewrite this title entirely. Here's why ___."

These honesty patterns are often the most converting lines on the page. They lower the buyer's defenses.

## When the creator's existing copy is *already* good

Don't fix what's working. Quote the strong line back to them, name *why* it works, and tell them to keep it. Then focus surgical edits where the copy is actually weak. The audit is allowed to recommend "leave this alone".
