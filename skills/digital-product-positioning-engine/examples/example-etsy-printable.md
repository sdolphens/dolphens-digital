# Worked Example — Etsy Wall-Art Printable (Targeted Module)

This is what a **Targeted Module** request looks like — the creator only wants title + thumbnail repositioning, not a full audit. Shows how to honor scope while still bringing strategic depth.

---

**Inputs (fictional):**

- Product type: digital wall-art print (PDF, 5 sizes)
- Title: *Minimalist Boho Wall Art Printable — Modern Home Decor — Digital Download — Aesthetic Print*
- Platform: Etsy
- Price: $4.50
- Tone requested: warm & supportive
- Main frustration: "decent traffic, almost no clicks"

Request: *"Don't do the whole thing — just fix my title and thumbnail."*

---

Mode: Targeted Module — Title + Thumbnail Repositioning for *Minimalist Boho Wall Art Printable*.

> Quick note before I rewrite: your traffic is fine — your *click-through* is the problem. That's a thumbnail issue more than a title issue. I'll fix both, but if you only do one this week, do the thumbnail.

## Why the current setup is invisible

- The title stacks five generic descriptors (*minimalist*, *boho*, *modern*, *aesthetic*, *digital download*). Etsy's algorithm sees keywords; humans see noise. None of these words distinguish you from ten thousand other listings.
- "Aesthetic" has become a non-word in this category. It signals nothing.
- I'm assuming the thumbnail is the print rendered on a beige wall with a small plant nearby. Tell me if I'm wrong — but if I'm right, it's wearing the exact uniform of its competitors.

## Thumbnail concepts (5)

> Pick concepts 1 or 4 to test first. Both create contrast in the Etsy grid without breaking the aesthetic of the print itself.

**Concept 1 — In a real room, not a staged wall (identity match + context + quiet)**
*Visual:* the print framed, hanging above an actual reading chair in a real apartment. Late-afternoon light. Some "lived-in" detail in frame — a folded throw, an open book on the chair.
*Emotional framing:* this is what your living room could feel like.
*Curiosity hook:* the print is partly cropped by the frame edge.
*Text overlay:* none.
*Click-trigger:* identity match — the buyer sees the room they want to live in.

**Concept 2 — Two-print pairing (pattern interrupt + product + medium)**
*Visual:* two prints in a vertical pairing, one above the other, on a single wall. Implies "this works as a set".
*Click-trigger:* upsell-prep + pattern interrupt (most competitors show one).

**Concept 3 — Frame-and-shadow (premium + quiet)**
*Visual:* the print in a thin black frame, soft daylight shadow falling across it. Negative space dominant.
*Emotional framing:* gallery-quiet.
*Click-trigger:* price-zone signal — buyer perceives the print as more premium than $4.50.

**Concept 4 — The desk corner (identity match + context + medium)**
*Visual:* the print propped behind a real desk setup — a ceramic cup, a paperback, a notebook. No floral, no boho clichés.
*Click-trigger:* repositions the print from "boho wall art" to "calm-desk art" — a niche-collision move.

**Concept 5 — Type-led restraint (status + quiet)**
*Visual:* the print held in hand against a plain wall, the buyer's hand cropped in frame. Implies "real person, real wall".
*Click-trigger:* identity match — counters the staged-flatlay aesthetic.

**Visual hierarchy notes:** one print, one focal point, one shadow. Drop the third decorative object you're almost certainly tempted to add.

**Anti-patterns:** the beige wall + tiny green plant + macrame combo; the four-print grid collage; bright Pinterest-template overlay text.

## What I deliberately didn't do

You asked for title + thumbnail, so I didn't touch your description or pricing. Two flags for when you're ready:

- $4.50 is in a race-to-bottom zone for this category. Concept 3 (frame-and-shadow) plus a tighter title would support $7–9 without losing the audience.
- "Boho" in the listing tags may be costing you clicks from the buyer who's *actively avoiding* boho. Worth testing tag changes when you swap the title.
